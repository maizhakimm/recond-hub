When a car is sold at a Japanese auction house such as USS, TAA, JU, CAA or HAA, an independent inspector checks it and writes an **auction sheet** (in Japanese, 出品票). It is the car's report card, and for a recond buyer in Malaysia it is the best evidence you have of the car's condition before it left Japan.

Every car at our showrooms is sold with its original sheet. Here is how to read one.

<Callout title="Quick rule">
Grade 4 or higher, interior A or B, and a damage map with only small letters and numbers: that is a car you can buy with confidence.
</Callout>

## The overall grade

The large number, usually at the top right, is the **overall grade**. It summarises the whole car.

| Grade | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| S or 6 | Brand new or under 1 year old with very low mileage |
| 5 | Excellent, almost new condition |
| 4.5 | Very good, minor marks only |
| 4 | Good, a few small scratches or dents |
| 3.5 | Average, visible wear or several marks |
| 3 and below | Poor, significant wear or damage |
| R / RA | Repaired accident damage (structure) |
| 0 or *** | Flood, fire or major damage |

Most recond cars sold in Malaysia are grade 4 to 5.

## The interior grade

Next to the overall grade is a letter for the **interior**: A (like new), B (clean, light wear), C (stains or wear), D (poor) and E (very poor). For a family MPV like the Alphard or Vellfire, interior B or better is what you want.

## Mileage and year

The sheet shows the odometer reading at auction in kilometres. Compare it with the mileage on the car today. The difference should be small, just the distance from auction to port and from port to showroom.

<Callout title="Mileage tip">
If the sheet shows 30,000 km but the car's dashboard shows 18,000 km, walk away. Always cross-check with the export certificate.
</Callout>

## The damage map

The drawing of the car shows where the inspector found marks. Each mark is a letter plus a number for size (1 is small, 3 is large):

- **A**: scratch (A1 small, A3 large)
- **U**: dent
- **B**: dent with scratch
- **W**: wave or repaired panel
- **S**: rust
- **C**: corrosion
- **P**: paint mark or repaint
- **X**: panel needs replacing
- **XX**: panel already replaced
- **Y**: crack or hole

A few A1 and U1 marks are normal on any used car. Lots of W, XX or S marks mean repair work and are worth asking about.

## Inspector's notes

The written comments are often the most useful part: they mention things like "sunroof", "pilot seats", "smell of smoke", "engine noise" or "windscreen chip". We translate these for you on request.

## How to verify a sheet

1. Ask for the **auction house and lot number**.
2. Check that the chassis number and mileage match the car and its **export certificate**.
3. Buy from a dealer who shows the sheet openly. On our site you can view it on every car page.

Once you can read the sheet, you can compare two cars at the same price and know which is really the better buy.
